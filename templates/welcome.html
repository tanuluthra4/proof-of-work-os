How it works

  1. Create a task and attach evidence. Repository links, demos, pull requests, write-ups.
  2. Choose your reviewers. Pick one to five reviewer accounts and how many must approve (for example 2 of 3).
  3. Reviewers decide independently. Each records approve or reject with a written note. They cannot see each other’s decisions until they have decided.
  4. Share the result. A verified proof can be shared by a private link that you can revoke at any time.

Verification process

Every proof moves through a fixed state machine: created, in progress, evidence required, evidence submitted, under review, then verified or rejected. The server refuses any step that is not allowed and records the refusal. A proof becomes verified only when the required number of distinct reviewers approve; it is rejected as soon as that number can no longer be reached. Changing evidence starts a new review round, and earlier decisions stay in the history.

The history is hash-chained, so later edits are detectable (tamper-evident, not tamper-proof).

FAQ

Does “verified” mean an organisation certified the developer?

No. It means the named reviewer accounts approved the submitted evidence. Reviewer identity and expertise are not independently verified, and several reviewers are not an organisation.

Can a developer approve their own work?

No. Reviewers must be other reviewer accounts, and reviewer sign-up is invitation-only.

Who can see my proof?

Only you and your assigned reviewers, unless you create a public link. Public pages show reviewer usernames, decisions and notes the reviewer marked public, never e-mails or private notes.

What if I change my evidence after submitting?

Withdraw the submission. A new round starts and earlier approvals no longer count, but they stay in the history.

Is the audit trail tamper-proof?

No, it is tamper-evident. Anyone with database access could rewrite it and its hashes together; the check detects ordinary edits and inconsistencies.
